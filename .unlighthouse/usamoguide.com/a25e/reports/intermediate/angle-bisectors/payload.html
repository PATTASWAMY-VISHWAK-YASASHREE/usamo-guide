Overview

Angle bisectors turn angles into side ratios. You will need them for area and segment-ratio problems, and frequently appear in AMC 8, AMC 10, and basic olympiad geometry.

The most important result is the Angle Bisector Theorem, which changes angle information into side-length information. This makes many geometry problems simple algebra.

Definitions and Key Ideas

Consider triangle ABCABCABC, and let the bisector of ∠A\angle A∠A intersect side BCBCBC at DDD.

The Angle Bisector Theorem states that

BDDC=ABAC.\frac{BD}{DC} = \frac{AB}{AC}.DCBD​=ACAB​.

In words, an angle bisector divides the opposite side in the ratio of the adjacent sides.

Because triangles ABDABDABD and ACDACDACD share the same altitude from AAA, their areas satisfy

[ABD][ACD]=BDDC.\frac{[ABD]}{[ACD]} = \frac{BD}{DC}.[ACD][ABD]​=DCBD​.

Combining this with the Angle Bisector Theorem gives

[ABD][ACD]=ABAC.\frac{[ABD]}{[ACD]} = \frac{AB}{AC}.[ACD][ABD]​=ACAB​.

This is one of the fastest shortcuts for area-ratio problems.

Another useful fact is that every interior angle bisector meets the opposite side between its endpoints, dividing the triangle into two smaller triangles.

The Angle Bisector Theorem

If ADADAD bisects ∠A\angle A∠A in triangle ABCABCABC, then

BDDC=ABAC.\frac{BD}{DC} = \frac{AB}{AC}.DCBD​=ACAB​.

This theorem is often used together with

BD+DC=BCBD+DC=BCBD+DC=BC

to find unknown segment lengths.

For example, if

AB=8,AC=6,AB=8,\qquad AC=6,AB=8,AC=6,

then

BD:DC=8:6=4:3.BD:DC=8:6=4:3.BD:DC=8:6=4:3.

If BC=14BC=14BC=14, then

BD=8,DC=6.BD=8,\qquad DC=6.BD=8,DC=6.

Area Ratios

One of the most common AMC applications of angle bisectors involves areas.

Since triangles ABDABDABD and ACDACDACD share the same altitude from AAA,

[ABD][ACD]=BDDC.\frac{[ABD]}{[ACD]} = \frac{BD}{DC}.[ACD][ABD]​=DCBD​.

Using the Angle Bisector Theorem,

[ABD][ACD]=ABAC.\frac{[ABD]}{[ACD]} = \frac{AB}{AC}.[ACD][ABD]​=ACAB​.

Thus, if

AB:AC=3:5,AB:AC=3:5,AB:AC=3:5,

then

[ABD]:[ACD]=3:5.[ABD]:[ACD]=3:5.[ABD]:[ACD]=3:5.

No area formulas are required.

Recognizing Angle Bisector Problems

Common clues that suggest using the Angle Bisector Theorem include:

  • A segment is explicitly described as an angle bisector.
  • Two side lengths adjacent to an angle are given.
  • A ratio on the opposite side is requested.
  • Area ratios appear alongside an angle bisector.
  • A triangle is divided into two smaller triangles by a segment from a vertex.

Whenever you see an angle bisector and side lengths together, try the theorem immediately.

Common Pitfalls

  • Using the theorem on a median instead of an angle bisector.
  • Mixing up side lengths and segment lengths.
  • Writing
BDDC=BCAC\frac{BD}{DC} = \frac{BC}{AC}DCBD​=ACBC​

instead of

BDDC=ABAC.\frac{BD}{DC} = \frac{AB}{AC}.DCBD​=ACAB​.
  • Forgetting to use
BD+DC=BCBD+DC=BCBD+DC=BC

when the entire side length is known.

  • Applying the theorem without first confirming that the segment is actually an angle bisector.

Worked Example

In triangle ABCABCABC, AB=8AB=8AB=8, AC=6AC=6AC=6, and BC=7BC=7BC=7. The bisector from AAA meets BCBCBC at DDD. Find BDBDBD.

BDDC=86=43.\frac{BD}{DC} = \frac{8}{6} = \frac{4}{3}.DCBD​=68​=34​.

Since

BD+DC=7,BD+DC=7,BD+DC=7,

the segments are 444 and 333, so

BD=4.BD=4.BD=4.

More Examples

Example 1: Quick Ratio

In triangle ABCABCABC, AB=5AB=5AB=5 and AC=10AC=10AC=10. The angle bisector from AAA meets BCBCBC at DDD. Find BD:DCBD:DCBD:DC.

By the Angle Bisector Theorem,

BD:DC=AB:AC=1:2.BD:DC = AB:AC = 1:2.BD:DC=AB:AC=1:2.

Example 2: Area Ratio

If

[ABD]=12and[ACD]=18,[ABD]=12\qquad\text{and}\qquad[ACD]=18,[ABD]=12and[ACD]=18,

find BD:DCBD:DCBD:DC.

The triangles share an altitude from AAA, so

BD:DC=12:18=2:3.BD:DC = 12:18 = 2:3.BD:DC=12:18=2:3.

Example 3: Finding Segments

In triangle ABCABCABC, AB=12AB=12AB=12, AC=18AC=18AC=18, and BC=20BC=20BC=20. The angle bisector from AAA meets BCBCBC at DDD. Find DCDCDC.

The Angle Bisector Theorem gives

BD:DC=12:18=2:3.BD:DC = 12:18 = 2:3.BD:DC=12:18=2:3.

Since

BD+DC=20,BD+DC=20,BD+DC=20,

the side is divided into 555 equal parts.

Each part has length 444, so

DC=12.DC=12.DC=12.

Example 4: Area From Side Ratios

In triangle DIHDIHDIH, the angle bisector from DDD meets IHIHIH at CCC. Suppose

DI=9,DH=6,DI=9,\qquad DH=6,DI=9,DH=6,

and

[DIH]=30.[DIH]=30.[DIH]=30.

Find [DIC][DIC][DIC].

Since

DI:DH=9:6=3:2,DI:DH=9:6=3:2,DI:DH=9:6=3:2,

we have

[DIC]:[DHC]=3:2.[DIC]:[DHC]=3:2.[DIC]:[DHC]=3:2.

The total area is 303030, so

[DIC]=35⋅30=18.[DIC] = \frac{3}{5}\cdot30 = 18.[DIC]=53​⋅30=18.

Strategy Checklist

  • Confirm the segment is an angle bisector.
  • Immediately write
BDDC=ABAC.\frac{BD}{DC} = \frac{AB}{AC}.DCBD​=ACAB​.
  • Use
BD+DC=BCBD+DC=BCBD+DC=BC

when a full side length is given.

  • Convert area information into side ratios whenever possible.

  • Look for shared altitudes when comparing triangle areas.
